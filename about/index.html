About¶

Extracting Historical Semantic Knowledge & Repurposing It for Exact Symbolic Reasoning

About This Edition¶

geometor.byrne brings Oliver Byrne's celebrated 1847 Chiswick Press masterpiece—The First Six Books of the Elements of Euclid in Which Coloured Diagrams and Symbols Are Used Instead of Letters for the Greater Ease of Learners—into the GEOMETOR Digital Scriptorium as both a scholarly multi-track Book Codex (Byrne 1847 Edition · Executive Summary · Scholarly & Design Notes) and an executable Pointer-Free Visual Pseudocode laboratory.

Where classical Euclidean prose (T. L. Heath, geometor/euclid) operates by reference—relying on arbitrary alphabetic vertex pointers ($A, B, C, \dots$) requiring the reader’s working memory to shuttle continually between text and diagram—Byrne formulated a revolutionary pass-by-value geometry. As Byrne observed on page ix of his 1847 Introduction: “the parts being differently coloured, are made to name themselves, for their forms in corresponding colours represent them in the demonstration.” This edition extracts Byrne’s implicit visual syntax into a formal, typed geometric token grammar (geometor.byrne.pseudocode) connected directly to geometor/elements and geometor/codes.

Every showcase plate (from Proposition I.1 through VI.30) is reconstructed from scratch in geometor.byrne.constructions using geometor.model.Model (zero floating-point coordinates) over exact algebraic number fields ($\mathbb{Q}$, $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{3})$, $\mathbb{Q}(\phi)$, and $\mathbb{Q}(\phi, \sqrt{10 - 2\sqrt{5}})$), with proof invariants verified symbolically via Rational Trigonometry quadrance and spread laws. A custom Sphinx extension renders interactive inline SVG micro-glyphs (, , , ) wired directly to responsive .euclid-graphic plates.

The GEOMETOR Mission¶

The GEOMETOR initiative, founded by phi ARCHITECT, is dedicated to a three-fold synthesis of classical geometry, exact symbolic computation, and modern knowledge architecture:

  • Extracting Historical Semantic Knowledge: Moving beyond passive page scans and static OCR to recover the living deductive structure of 2,300+ years of geometric, axiomatic, and philosophical treatises. We elevate running prose, marginal citations, primary-source folios (via publish folio), and calibrated Ink-to-Vector .euclid-graphic diagrams into structured, machine-addressable semantic graphs where every enunciation, proof step, and historical cross-reference is individually referenceable.

  • Exact Symbolic Grounding (``geometor.model``): Anchoring geometric constructions in 100% exact symbolic algebra (via SymPy) and Rational Trigonometry invariants (quadrance, spread, cross-ratio) over exact algebraic number fields rather than lossy floating-point approximations. Every geometric theorem is treated as an exact algebraic truth verifiable through computational modeling.

  • Repurposing Knowledge Across Mediums: Re-deploying extracted semantic and algebraic structures across synchronized multi-track Book Codices and EPUB 3 editions (via photon-platform/publish), computable deductive canons (geometor.elements, geometor.etc, geometor.pappus, geometor.divine), interactive SVG laboratories (geometor.explorer), QHD visual broadcasts, and neuro-symbolic program synthesis (geometor.seer) across geometor.com.

Sources, Provenance & Methodology¶

All transcriptions, color/stroke assignments, and symbolic SVG plates derive strictly from the 100% public-domain 1847 William Pickering / Charles Whittingham print:

  • Primary Print Copy: Oliver Byrne, The First Six Books of the Elements of Euclid in Which Coloured Diagrams and Symbols Are Used Instead of Letters for the Greater Ease of Learners. London: William Pickering, printed by Charles Whittingham at the Chiswick Press, 1847. Verified against high-resolution scans from the Thomas Fisher Rare Book Library (Internet Archive firstsixbooksofe00eucl).

  • Scholarly Appreciation: In our bibliography and historical commentary we honor Ruari McLean (1963), Edward Tufte (1990), and Nicholas Rougeux (c82.net/euclid, 2018; Making of Byrne's Euclid), while maintaining a strict zero-copy copyright boundary with modern third-party web and print editions.

The text and diagrams are collated and structured into the Digital Scriptorium multi-track codex format, cross-referenced with the classical Greek text of Euclid and modern formal mathematical foundations.
