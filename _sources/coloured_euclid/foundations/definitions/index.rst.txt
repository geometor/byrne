Book I Definitions (I–XXXV)
===========================

.. rubric:: Robert Simson's 35 Definitions in Chiswick Press Register (pp. xviii–xxii, Leaves 22–26)

.. picture:: 0022.g1.png
   :alt: Book I Definitions parallel lines and rectilinear plates on Leaf 22 (p. xviii).
   :caption: **Book I Definitions Plate Extract (Leaf 22, p. xviii).** Vertical strip of inline four-color definition figures.

* **I.** A *point* is that which has no parts, or which has no magnitude.
* **II.** A *line* (:byrne-line:`black solid`) is length without breadth.
* **III.** The extremities of a line are points.
* **IV.** A *straight* or *right line* (:byrne-line:`red solid`) is that which lies evenly between its extreme points.
* **V–VII.** A *superficies* (surface) has length and breadth only; its extremities are lines; a *plane superficies* is that in which any two points being taken, the straight line between them lies wholly in that plane.

.. folio:: 0023
   :page: xix

* **VIII–IX.** A *plane angle* (:byrne-angle:`blue`) is the inclination of two lines to one another in a plane, which meet together, but are not in the same direction; a *plane rectilineal angle* is contained by two straight lines.
* **X.** When a straight line standing on another straight line makes the adjacent angles equal (:byrne-angle:`yellow` = :byrne-angle:`red`), each is called a *right angle* (:byrne-op:`right-angle`), and the standing line is *perpendicular* (:byrne-op:`perp`) to the other.
* **XI–XII.** An *obtuse angle* is greater (:byrne-op:`gt`) than a right angle; an *acute angle* is less (:byrne-op:`lt`) than a right angle.
* **XIII–XIV.** A *term* or *boundary* is the extremity of anything; a *figure* is that which is enclosed by one or more boundaries.

.. folio:: 0024
   :page: xx

* **XV–XVIII.** A *circle* (:byrne-circle:`blue`) is a plane figure contained by one line (the *circumference*) such that all straight lines from the *centre* to the circumference are equal; a *diameter* passes through the centre and bisects the circle into two *semicircles*.
* **XIX–XXIII.** A *segment of a circle* is contained by a straight line and a part of the circumference; rectilineal figures are *trilateral* (:byrne-tri:`yellow filled`), *quadrilateral* (:byrne-sq:`red filled`), or *multilateral*.

.. folio:: 0025
   :page: xxi

* **XXIV–XXVI.** Among trilateral figures: an *equilateral triangle* has three equal sides; an *isosceles triangle* has two equal sides; a *scalene triangle* has three unequal sides; further classed as *right-angled*, *obtuse-angled*, and *acute-angled*.
* **XXVII–XXXII.** Among four-sided figures (:person:`Simson's <simson>` subdivision): **XXVII.** a *square* (:byrne-sq:`black filled`) has all sides equal and all angles right; **XXVIII.** an *oblong* (:byrne-rect:`yellow filled`) is right-angled without equal sides; **XXIX.** a *rhombus* is equilateral without right angles; **XXX.** a *rhomboid* has opposite sides and angles equal; **XXXI–XXXII.** *trapezia* and *parallelograms*.

.. folio:: 0026
   :page: xxii

* **XXXIII–XXXIV.** *Rhomboids* and *trapezia* diagrams on p. xxii.
* **XXXV.** *Parallel straight lines* (:byrne-op:`parallel`) are such as are in the same plane, and which being produced ever so far both ways do not meet.
