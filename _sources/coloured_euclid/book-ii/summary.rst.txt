Book II Summary: Geometric Algebra Without Symbolic Variables
=============================================================

.. rubric:: How Byrne's Colored Areas Realize Book II Polynomial Identities

.. folio:: 0085
   :page: 51

Book II of Euclid's *Elements* establishes quadratic identities—distributivity (``II.1``), $(a+b)^2$ (:ref:`II.4 <byrne-ii-4>`), difference of squares and completing the square (:ref:`II.5 <byrne-ii-5>`–``II.6``), the Law of Cosines (``II.12``–``II.13``), and quadrature of any rectilinear figure (``II.14``)—entirely through **planar dissection** without numerical coordinates or ratio theory:

* In :ref:`II.4 <byrne-ii-4>`, Byrne's diagonal half-filled squares (:byrne-sq:`blue half`, :byrne-sq:`red half`) visually separate the quadratic terms $a^2, b^2$ from the solid yellow cross-terms $2ab$ (:byrne-rect:`yellow filled`).
* In :ref:`II.5 <byrne-ii-5>`, gnomon surgery (:byrne-rect:`black filled` $\mapsto$ :byrne-rect:`yellow filled`) makes completing the square visually immediate.
* In :ref:`II.11 <byrne-ii-11>`, Euclid solves $x^2 + a x = a^2$ (cutting $a$ in :term:`extreme and mean ratio <extreme-and-mean-ratio>`, $x = a / \phi$) by subtracting a shared rectangular strip from two equal composite regions—feeding directly into the Golden Triangle (:ref:`IV.10 <byrne-iv-10>`) and regular pentagon (:ref:`IV.11 <byrne-iv-11>`).
