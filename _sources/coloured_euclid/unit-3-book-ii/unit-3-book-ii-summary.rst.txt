Unit III Summary: Geometric Algebra Without Symbolic Variables
==============================================================

.. rubric:: How Byrne's Colored Areas Realize Book II Polynomial Identities

Book II of Euclid's *Elements* establishes quadratic identities—distributivity (``II.1``), $(a+b)^2$ (``II.4``), difference of squares and completing the square (``II.5``–``II.6``), the Law of Cosines (``II.12``–``II.13``), and quadrature of any rectilinear figure (``II.14``)—entirely through **planar dissection** without numerical coordinates or ratio theory:

* In ``II.4``, Byrne's diagonal half-filled squares (:byrne-sq:`blue half`, :byrne-sq:`red half`) visually separate the quadratic terms $a^2, b^2$ from the solid yellow cross-terms $2ab$ (:byrne-rect:`yellow filled`).
* In ``II.11``, Euclid solves $x^2 + a x = a^2$ (cutting $a$ in :term:`extreme and mean ratio <extreme-and-mean-ratio>`, $x = a / \phi$) by subtracting a shared rectangular strip from two equal composite regions—a proof that feeds directly into the $36^\circ\text{–}72^\circ\text{–}72^\circ$ Golden Triangle (``IV.10``) and regular pentagon (``IV.11``).
