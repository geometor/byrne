Unit I Summary: Foundations & Ocular Grammar
============================================

.. rubric:: Executive Synthesis of Byrne's Preliminary Apparatus (pp. vii–xxix)

1. **The Pedagogical Thesis (pp. vii–xvi)**:
   :person:`Oliver Byrne <byrne>` argues that Euclidean geometry appears arduous to beginners not because its spatial reasoning is obscure, but because *alphabetic vertex naming* interposes an artificial verbal cipher between eye and diagram. By making colored lines, angles, and planar regions *name themselves*, deductive inference operates directly on visual primitives.
2. **Simson's Axiomatic Base (pp. xvii–xxiv)**:
   Byrne retains the rigorous deductive skeleton of :person:`Robert Simson's <simson>` :cite:`1756 Glasgow edition <simson:1756>`: **35 Definitions** (subdividing quadrilaterals up to ``Def. 27`` square and ``Def. 35`` parallels), **3 Constructive Postulates** (straightedge join, straightedge extension, compass circle), and **12 Axioms** (relocating Euclid's Fourth and Fifth Postulates to ``Axiom XI`` and ``Axiom XII``).
3. **Algebraic Compression of Prose (pp. xxv–xxix)**:
   Through his *Elucidations* and *Symbols and Abbreviations*, Byrne equips Euclidean deduction with a compact operator vocabulary (``=``, ``+``, ``-``, :byrne-op:`gt`, :byrne-op:`lt`, :byrne-op:`parallel`, :byrne-op:`perp`, :byrne-op:`right-angle`, :byrne-op:`two-right-angles`, :byrne-op:`therefore`, :byrne-op:`because`), transforming dense paragraphs into scannable vertical equations.
