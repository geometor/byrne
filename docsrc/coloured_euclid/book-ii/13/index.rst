Proposition II.13 — Acute-Triangle Law of Cosines
=================================================

.. rubric:: Book II, Proposition XIII. Theorem (*Pickering pp. 68–69*)

IN any triangle, the square of the side subtending an acute angle, is less than the sum of the squares of the sides containing that angle, by twice the rectangle contained by either of these sides, and the part of it intercepted between the foot of the perpendicular let fall on it from the opposite angle, and the angular point of the acute angle. FIRST. :byrne-line:`blue solid squared` ⊐ (black-and-dotted line)² + :byrne-line:`red solid squared` by 2 · (black-and-dotted line) · (black-dotted line). SECOND. :byrne-line:`blue solid squared` ⊐ :byrne-line:`red solid squared` + :byrne-line:`black solid squared` by 2 · :byrne-line:`black solid` · (black-and-dotted line).
