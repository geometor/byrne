Why Byrne Invented Half-Filled Squares in ``II.4``
--------------------------------------------------

Inspection of Pickering **pp. 56–57 (Leaves 90–91)** shows a striking Chiswick Press graphic device: instead of printing the two diagonal sub-squares in solid ink, :person:`Byrne <byrne>` fills only the upper-left triangle of each square while outlining the boundary (:byrne-sq:`blue half`, :byrne-sq:`red half`). Because Euclid first proves in :ref:`II.4 <byrne-ii-4>` that the two sub-triangles cut by the diagonal are isosceles (:just:`I.6`) before concluding that the four-sided figures are squares, half-filling makes both the constituent triangle *and* the enclosing square simultaneously legible in a single woodblock impression.

Pre-Proportional Golden Section in :math:`\mathbb{Q}(\phi)`
-----------------------------------------------------------

Book II precedes Eudoxian ratio theory (Book V), so Euclid formulates the Golden Section in :ref:`II.11 <byrne-ii-11>` purely as an equality of colored planar regions (:byrne-sq:`blue filled` = :byrne-rect:`yellow filled`), later translated into continuous proportion in :ref:`VI.30 <byrne-vi-30>`.
