Book VI — Similar Figures & Extreme and Mean Ratio
==================================================

.. rubric:: Proportional Geometry & Application of Areas (*Pickering pp. 213–268, Leaves 247–302*)

Book VI Key Definitions (p. 213)
--------------------------------

* **Definition I.** *Similar rectilineal figures* are those which have their several angles equal, each to each, and the sides about the equal angles proportionals (:byrne-op:`prop`).
* **Definition III.** A straight line is said to be *cut in extreme and mean ratio* (:term:`Golden Section <extreme-and-mean-ratio>`) when the whole line is to the greater segment as the greater segment is to the less.

Deductive Arc of Book VI
------------------------

* **Props. VI.1–VI.2 (``G.286``–``G.287``):** Triangles of the same altitude are to one another as their bases; a line drawn parallel (:byrne-op:`parallel`) to one side of a triangle cuts the other two sides proportionally, and conversely.
* **Prop. VI.3 (``G.288``):** Angle Bisector Theorem—bisecting an angle of a triangle divides the opposite base proportionally to the adjacent sides.
* **Prop. VI.13 (``G.298``):** Constructing the geometric mean $\sqrt{ab}$ between two segments via Thales's semicircle (:ref:`III.31 <byrne-iii-31>`).
* **Prop. VI.30 (:ref:`dedicated plate below <byrne-vi-30>`, ``G.328``):** Cutting a given finite straight line in extreme and mean ratio via hyperbolic application of areas (``VI.29``).
* **Prop. VI.31 (p. 259 · ``G.329``):** Generalized Pythagorean Theorem—extending :ref:`I.47 <byrne-i-47>` from squares to *any three similar and similarly described rectilineal figures* on the sides of a right-angled triangle.
