Solving the Five-Edge Pentagon Paradox on a Four-Ink Press
----------------------------------------------------------

:ref:`Proposition IV.11 <byrne-iv-11>` (Pickering **p. 137, Leaf 171**) poses a sharp combinatorial test for :person:`Oliver Byrne <byrne>`: a regular pentagon has **five** distinct perimeter chords, yet the Chiswick Press palette has only **four** inks (red, blue, yellow, black), and the inscribed Golden Triangle already occupies solid **yellow** (:byrne-tri:`yellow filled`)!

Byrne solves this :term:`register pressure <register-pressure>` cleanly: four sides receive solid inks (:byrne-line:`red solid`, :byrne-line:`black solid`, :byrne-line:`yellow solid`, :byrne-line:`blue solid`), and the fifth side is printed as **red dotted** (:byrne-line:`red dotted`), keeping all five chords individually addressable in the ``Q.E.F.`` conclusion without adding a fifth press run.

Archival Typographic Detail on Pickering p. 129
-----------------------------------------------

Direct scan inspection of Pickering **p. 129 (Leaf 163, Prop. IV.4)** reveals an authentic 1847 Chiswick Press compositorial slip in the italicized first line beside the woodcut initial ``I``: *"IN a given triangte [sic] ... to inscribe a circle."*
