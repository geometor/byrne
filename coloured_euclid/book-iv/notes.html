Solving the Five-Edge Pentagon Paradox on a Four-Ink Press¶

Proposition IV.11 (Pickering p. 137, Leaf 171) poses a sharp combinatorial test for Oliver Byrne: a regular pentagon has five distinct perimeter chords, yet the Chiswick Press palette has only four inks (red, blue, yellow, black), and the inscribed Golden Triangle already occupies solid yellow ()!

Byrne solves this register pressure cleanly: four sides receive solid inks (, , , ), and the fifth side is printed as red dotted (), keeping all five chords individually addressable in the Q.E.F. conclusion without adding a fifth press run.

Archival Typographic Detail on Pickering p. 129¶

Direct scan inspection of Pickering p. 129 (Leaf 163, Prop. IV.4) reveals an authentic 1847 Chiswick Press compositorial slip in the italicized first line beside the woodcut initial I: “IN a given triangte [sic] … to inscribe a circle.”
