Book V — Color-Coded Symbolic Algebra of Eudoxian Proportions¶

Abstract Magnitude Tokens & Equimultiple Calculus (Pickering pp. 145–210, Leaves 179–244)

In Book V, Eudoxus and Euclid generalize ratio and proportion from spatial lines to arbitrary continuous magnitudes of the same kind—lengths, planar areas, polyhedral volumes, or angles. Recognizing that line diagrams obscure this universality, Oliver Byrne abandons spatial figures across pages 153–212 and introduces typed colored magnitude symbols (, , , ).

Definitions & Axioms in Book V¶

Book V Definitions I–IV (Part, Multiple & Ratio)

I. A less magnitude is said to be an aliquot part or submultiple of a greater magnitude, when the less measures the greater; that is, when the less is contained a certain number of times exactly in the greater.

Proposition · Commentary

Book V Axioms (I–III, Simson Numbering)

I. Equimultiples or equisubmultiples of the same, or of equal magnitudes, are equal to one another.

Proposition · Commentary

Book V Definition V — Criterion of Same Ratio

Four magnitudes (, , , ) are said to be proportionals, or in the same ratio, when any equimultiples whatsoever be taken of the first and third, and any equimultiples whatsoever of the second and fourth: if the multiple of the first be greater than (⊏), equal to (=), or less than (⊐) the multiple of the second, then the multiple of the third is also greater than, equal to, or less than that of the fourth ([V.def.5]).

Proposition · Commentary

Book V Definition VII — Criterion of Greater Ratio

When of the equimultiples of four magnitudes (taken as in Definition V), the multiple of the first is greater (⊏) than that of the second, while the multiple of the third is not greater than that of the fourth, then the first is said to have to the second a greater ratio than the third magnitude has to the fourth ([V.def.7]).

Proposition · Commentary

Book V Definition XIII — Permutando (Alternando)

The technical term permutando, or alternando (by permutation or alternately), is used when there are four proportionals, and it is inferred that the first has the same ratio to the third which the second has to the fourth (Prop. V.16).

Proposition · Commentary

Book V Definition XVI — Dividendo

Dividendo (by division) is used when there are four proportionals, and it is inferred that the excess of the first above the second is to the second as the excess of the third above the fourth is to the fourth (Prop. V.17). Note that Byrne prints Def. XVI immediately prior to V.17.

Proposition · Commentary

Book V Definition XV — Componendo

Componendo (by composition) is used when there are four proportionals, and it is inferred that the first together with the second is to the second as the third together with the fourth is to the fourth (Prop. V.18). Note that Byrne prints Def. XV immediately prior to V.18.

Proposition · Commentary

Book V Definitions XIX–XX — Ex Aequali

Definition XIX (p. 188): Ex æquali (from equality of distance) used simply by itself, when the first magnitude is to the last of the first rank as the first is to the last of the second rank (Prop. V.22).

Proposition · Commentary

Book V Definitions X, XI & A — Duplicate, Triplicate & Compound Ratio

Definition X (p. 200): When three magnitudes are continual proportionals, the first is said to have to the third the duplicate ratio of that which it has to the second.

Proposition · Commentary

Propositions in Book V¶

Proposition V.1 — Distributivity of Equimultiples Over Addition

If any number of magnitudes be equimultiples of as many others, each of each: what multiple soever any one of the first is of its part, the same multiple shall of the first magnitudes taken together be of all the others taken together.

Proposition · Commentary

Proposition V.2 — Sum of Equimultiples Is Same Multiple of Whole

If the first magnitude be the same multiple of the second that the third is of the fourth, and the fifth the same multiple of the second that the sixth is of the fourth, then shall the first, together with the fifth, be the same multiple of the second that the third, together with the sixth, is of the fourth.

Proposition · Commentary

Proposition V.3 — Equimultiples of Equimultiples Are Equimultiples

If the first of four magnitudes be the same multiple of the second that the third is of the fourth, and if any equimultiples whatever of the first and third be taken, those shall be equimultiples; one of the second, and the other of the fourth.

Proposition · Commentary

Proposition V.4 — Same Ratio Preserved Under Equimultiples

If the first of four magnitudes have the same ratio to the second, which the third has to the fourth, then any equimultiples whatever of the first and third shall have the same ratio to any equimultiples of the second and fourth; viz., the equimultiple of the first shall have the same ratio to that of the second, which the equimultiple of the third has to that of the fourth.

Proposition · Commentary

Proposition V.5 — Difference of Equimultiples Equals Equimultiple of Difference

If one magnitude be the same multiple of another, which a magnitude taken from the first is of a magnitude taken from the other, the remainder shall be the same multiple of the remainder, that the whole is of the whole.

Proposition · Commentary

Proposition V.6 — Subtraction of Multiples Leaves Multiple or Equality

If two magnitudes be equimultiples of two others, and if equimultiples of these be taken from the first two, the remainders are either equal to these others, or equimultiples of them.

Proposition · Commentary

Proposition V.A — Four Proportionals Preserve Order Relations (Inversion Prep.)

If the first of four magnitudes has the same ratio to the second which the third has to the fourth, then if the first be greater than the second, the third is also greater than the fourth; and if equal, equal; if less, less.

Proposition · Commentary

Proposition V.B — Invertendo (Inverted Proportion)

If four magnitudes are proportionals, they are proportionals also when taken inversely.

Proposition · Commentary

Proposition V.C — Equimultiple and Equisubmultiple Proportionality

If the first be the same multiple of the second, or the same part of it, that the third is of the fourth; the first is to the second, as the third is to the fourth.

Proposition · Commentary

Proposition V.D — Submultiple Proportionality from Same Ratio

If the first be to the second as the third to the fourth, and if the first be a multiple, or a part of the second; the third is the same multiple, or the same part of the fourth.

Proposition · Commentary

Proposition V.7 — Equal Magnitudes Have Same Ratio to the Same

Equal magnitudes have the same ratio to the same magnitude, and the same has the same ratio to equal magnitudes.

Proposition · Commentary

Proposition V.8 — Greater Magnitude Has Greater Ratio

Of unequal magnitudes the greater has a greater ratio to the same than the less has: and the same magnitude has a greater ratio to the less than it has to the greater.

Proposition · Commentary

Proposition V.9 — Magnitudes Having Same Ratio to the Same Are Equal

Magnitudes which have the same ratio to the same magnitude are equal to one another; and those to which the same magnitude has the same ratio are equal to one another.

Proposition · Commentary

Proposition V.10 — Magnitude Having Greater Ratio Is Greater

That magnitude which has a greater ratio than another has unto the same magnitude, is the greater of the two: and that magnitude to which the same has a greater ratio than it has unto another magnitude, is the less of the two.

Proposition · Commentary

Proposition V.11 — Transitivity of Ratios

Ratios that are the same to the same ratio, are the same to each other.

Proposition · Commentary

Proposition V.12 — Ratio of Sum of Antecedents to Sum of Consequents

If any number of magnitudes be proportionals, as one of the antecedents is to its consequent, so shall all the antecedents taken together be to all the consequents.

Proposition · Commentary

Proposition V.13 — Substitution Preserves Greater Ratio

If the first has to the second the same ratio which the third has to the fourth, but the third to the fourth a greater ratio than the fifth has to the sixth; the first shall also have to the second a greater ratio than the fifth to the sixth.

Proposition · Commentary

Proposition V.14 — Order Preservation Among Proportionals of Same Kind

If the first has to the second the same ratio which the third has to the fourth; then, if the first be greater than the third, the second shall be greater than the fourth; and if equal, equal; and if less, less.

Proposition · Commentary

Proposition V.15 — Magnitudes Have Same Ratio as Their Equimultiples

Magnitudes have the same ratio to one another which their equimultiples have.

Proposition · Commentary

Proposition V.16 — Alternando (Alternate Proportion)

If four magnitudes of the same kind be proportionals, they are also proportionals when taken alternately.

Proposition · Commentary

Proposition V.17 — Dividendo (Proportion by Division)

If magnitudes, taken jointly, be proportionals, they shall also be proportionals when taken separately: that is, if two magnitudes together have to one of them the same ratio which two others have to one of these, the remaining one of the first two shall have to the other the same ratio which the remaining one of the last two has to the other of these.

Proposition · Commentary

Proposition V.18 — Componendo (Joint Proportion)

If magnitudes, taken separately, be proportionals, they shall also be proportionals when taken jointly: that is, if the first be to the second as the third is to the fourth, the first and second together shall be to the second as the third and fourth together is to the fourth.

Proposition · Commentary

Proposition V.19 — Remainder of Wholes Is to Remainder as Whole to Whole

If a whole magnitude be to a whole, as a magnitude taken from the first, is to a magnitude taken from the other; the remainder shall be to the remainder, as the whole to the whole.

Proposition · Commentary

Proposition V.E — Convertendo (Proportion by Conversion)

If four magnitudes be proportionals, they are also proportionals by conversion: that is, the first is to its excess above the second, as the third to its excess above the fourth.

Proposition · Commentary

Proposition V.20 — Ex Aequali Order Preservation (Three Magnitudes)

If there be three magnitudes, and other three, which, taken two and two, have the same ratio; then, if the first be greater than the third, the fourth shall be greater than the sixth; and if equal, equal; and if less, less.

Proposition · Commentary

Proposition V.21 — Ex Aequali Perturbata Order Preservation (Three Magnitudes)

If there be three magnitudes, and other three which have the same ratio, taken two and two, but in a cross order; then if the first magnitude be greater than the third, the fourth shall be greater than the sixth; and if equal, equal; and if less, less.

Proposition · Commentary

Proposition V.22 — Ex Aequali (Equality of Distance)

If there be any number of magnitudes, and as many others, which, taken two and two in order, have the same ratio; the first shall have to the last of the first magnitudes the same ratio which the first of the others has to the last of the same.

Proposition · Commentary

Proposition V.23 — Ex Aequali in Perturbate Proportion

If there be any number of magnitudes, and as many others, which, taken two and two in a cross order, have the same ratio; the first shall have to the last of the first magnitudes the same ratio which the first of the others has to the last of the same. N.B.—This is usually cited by the words “ex æquali in proportione perturbatâ;” or “ex æquo perturbato.”

Proposition · Commentary

Proposition V.24 — Sum of First and Fifth Proportionals

If the first has to the second the same ratio which the third has to the fourth, and the fifth to the second the same which the sixth has to the fourth, the first and fifth together shall have to the second the same ratio which the third and sixth together have to the fourth.

Proposition · Commentary

Proposition V.25 — Greatest and Least of Four Proportionals Exceed Middle Pair

If four magnitudes of the same kind are proportionals, the greatest and least of them together are greater than the other two together.

Proposition · Commentary

Proposition V.F — Ratios Compounded of Equal Ratios Are Equal

Ratios which are compounded of the same ratios are the same to one another.

Proposition · Commentary

Proposition V.G — Composition of Multiple Equal Ratios

If several ratios be the same to several ratios, each to each, the ratio which is compounded of ratios which are the same to the first ratios, each to each, shall be the same to the ratio compounded of ratios which are the same to the other ratios, each to each.

Proposition · Commentary

Proposition V.H — Cancelation in Compounded Ratios

If a ratio which is compounded of several ratios be the same to a ratio which is compounded of several other ratios; and if one of the first ratios, or the ratio which is compounded of several of them, be the same to one of the last ratios, or to the ratio which is compounded of several of them; then the remaining ratio of the first, or, if there be more than one, the ratio compounded of the remaining ratios, shall be the same to the remaining ratio of the last, or, if there be more than one, to the ratio compounded of these remaining ratios.

Proposition · Commentary

Proposition V.K — Factoring and Reordering Compound Ratios

If there be any number of ratios, and any number of other ratios, such that the ratio which is compounded of ratios, which are the same to the first ratios, each to each, is the same to the ratio which is compounded of ratios, which are the same, each to each, to the last ratios—and if one of the first ratios, or the ratio which is compounded of ratios, which are the same to several of the first ratios, each to each, be the same to one of the last ratios, or to the ratio which is compounded of ratios, which are the same, each to each, to several of the last ratios—then the remaining ratio of the first; or, if there be more than one, the ratio which is compounded of ratios, which are the same, each to each, to the remaining ratios of the first, shall be the same to the remaining ratio of the last; or, if there be more than one, to the ratio which is compounded of ratios, which are the same, each to each, to these remaining ratios.

Proposition · Commentary
