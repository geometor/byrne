Book II — Geometric Algebra & The Golden Section¶

Coloured Rectangles, Gnomons & Extreme and Mean Ratio (Pickering pp. 51–71, Leaves 85–105)

Book II translates quadratic algebraic identities into direct visual area decompositions—partitioning squares, contained rectangles (×), and gnomons across the binomial square (II.4), difference of squares (II.5), and the Golden Section (II.11).

Book II Definitions: Contained Rectangles & The Gnomon (p. 51)¶

  • Definition I. Every right-angled parallelogram is said to be contained (×) by any two of the straight lines which contain one of the right angles.

  • Definition II. In every parallelogram, any of the parallelograms about a diameter, together with the two complements, is called a gnomon.

Propositions in Book II¶

Proposition II.4 — Binomial Square

If a straight line be divided into any two parts ( and ), the square on the whole line is equal to the squares on the two parts, together with twice the rectangle contained by the parts.

Proposition II.5 — Equal & Unequal Division of a Line

If a straight line be divided into two equal parts and also into two unequal parts, the rectangle contained by the unequal parts, together with the square on the line between the points of section, is equal to the square on the half.

Proposition II.11 — The Golden Section by Colored Areas

To divide a given straight line () into two parts, so that the rectangle contained by the whole and one of the parts may be equal to the square on the other part.
