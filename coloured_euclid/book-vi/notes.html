Book VI Notes: Application of Areas & Projective Cross-Repository Links¶

Historical & Symbolic Commentary on Book VI

Hyperbolic Application of Areas (VI.29 → VI.30)¶

As Proclus (Friedlein, geometor/proclus) and Heath (1908) explain, Greek application of areas (parabole, elleipsis, hyperbole) is the direct geometric ancestor of Apollonius's conic sections (geometor/apollonius). In VI.30, applying a rectangle of area $1$ to a unit segment with square excess ($\phi^{-1}(1 + \phi^{-1}) = 1$) solves $x(1 + x) = 1 \implies x = \phi^{-1} = \frac{\sqrt{5}-1}{2}$ in $\mathbb{Q}(\phi)$.

Connections to geometor/pappus & geometor/divine¶

Book VI’s intercept and angle-bisector proportions (VI.2–VI.3) form the metric gateway to projective cross-ratios, harmonic conjugates, and golden-section chains explored in geometor/pappus and geometor/divine.
